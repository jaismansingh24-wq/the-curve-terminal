    Stats and accounts

    0.9.4

    20 October 2026
    • TPO single prints are now the rows where only one letter printed with rows of two or more letters directly above and below them, so a single row counts too. One letter at the top or bottom of a TPO is a tail, and is no longer marked or carried forward as a single print.

    0.9.3

    19 October 2026
    • A TPO chart is now set by length: a TPO every 30 minutes, every 4 hours, each trading day, or any number of minutes, counted from the start of the trading day at 6:00 pm ET. Choosing a length sets its usual rows and letter time (30 minutes: rows of 1 tick, a letter each minute; 4 hours: 4 ticks, a letter each minute; a day: 4 ticks, a letter each 30 minutes), and each can then be changed in Style.
    • Every TPO is drawn, each labelled on the time axis with the time it started, New York time. The overnight and regular-hours labels are gone.
    • Auto rows, on at first: as you zoom out or squeeze the price scale, rows join (8, 20, 40 ticks and so on) so the letters stay readable, and they go back to the size you set as you zoom in. Turned off in Style, rows stay at your size.
    • The initial balance is the range of the hour from 9:30 am ET, shown on a TPO that holds that whole hour. A TPO chart left at the old settings becomes a day's TPO with rows of 4 ticks; one with your own rows and letter time keeps them.

    0.9.2

    12 October 2026
    • The mark on Home now sits at the centre of a turning sphere, drawn like a Dyson sphere: a tilted wireframe that turns once every five seconds, with small collector panels riding its equator and a second, steeper orbit. The mark itself is unchanged, and the sphere stays still when movement is reduced.

    0.9.1

    12 October 2026
    • The menu is the vertical list again, in a panel down the left side. The menu button, now under the title bar at the left, opens it and closes it; so does M.
    • The mark on Home prints itself again every ten seconds: the curve and its node fade, the three base ticks print one after another while the curve draws from the left, and the node settles at its end. Now and then a slow wave passes along the name in the title bar. Both stay still when movement is reduced.

    • Home shows your journal's figures (closed trades, win rate with its likely range, average per trade, profit factor and the largest drawdown, each with what it rests on), your research in the Folio (pages, words, marked lines, tags and the page you edited last), and NQ positioning from the copy of the CFTC's report that Atlas last kept. Home fetches nothing.

    0.8.0

    12 October 2026
    • Replay's order panel in colour: the Buy buttons green and the Sell buttons red, a long position green and a short one red, and the same in the right-click menu and the table of trades.
    • Charts start in new colours: white candles up, black candles down, white borders and wicks, on a plain black chart. Style changes the borders and wicks too, as well as everything else.
    • Drag a chart past the latest bar to leave room to its right.
    • A footprint keeps its latest bar in the middle of the chart, both ways, as the replay moves. Drag that bar out of view and the chart stays where you left it; Fit or a double click brings it back.
    • Footprint buys and sells as a full fill, shaded by size as before, or as a histogram, in Style.
    • Volume and delta profiles each choose their own range: the visible bars, each day, or each regular session. Thin lines mark each volume profile's point of control and the top and bottom of its value area; Style turns them off.

When a replay starts, the overnight session (from the 4:00 pm ET close to the 9:30 am open, in lower-case letters) is already built, and the regular session builds as you play. Single prints (runs of two or more rows where only one period traded) are marked, and so is the initial balance (the range of the regular session's first hour).
    • TPO in Style: the sessions (overnight and regular hours, regular hours only, or the full trading day), letters or blocks, the period, rows and value area, shading by period, single prints, the value area, the initial balance and the open each on or off, and the colour of each.

From a file of trades it is worked out from every trade; from a file of bars, from each bar's typical price, so it can differ a little from another platform's.

    0.7.0

    12 October 2026
    • Replay now reads files of trades as well as bars: MotiveWave's tick export (Data, Export, with Tick chosen) is read as it is. The replay then moves trade by trade, the clock shows the second, and orders fill at the trade that reaches them rather than on an assumed path through a bar.
    • A tick export only reaches back so far. Add an export to this file joins a later export of the same contract to the one you have, keeping one copy of the trades where the two overlap.
    • One, two (side by side or stacked) or four charts, each with its own timeframe and type, and its own toolbar. All of them follow the same replay.
    • Three chart types. Candles. Footprint: each bar's buys and sells at every price, from a file of trades, its busiest row boxed and its delta under it. TPO: each session's prices by period as letters, regular hours or the full session, with its point of control and value area.
    • Volume and delta: a volume pane, a delta pane (each bar's buys less sells, with a running total that starts again each trading day), and volume and delta profiles of the visible bars or of each session, with the point of control and value area marked.
    • Buys and sells are inferred: a trade at the ask counts as a buy, at the bid as a sell. The exchange does not report the side, so platforms that infer it differently show different deltas from the same trades, and Replay says so on the page. From a file of bars there is no footprint or delta, and a profile spreads each bar's volume evenly over its range.
    • Move a chart in any direction by dragging it. Drag the price axis to stretch prices and the time axis to stretch time; the wheel zooms time, and with Ctrl, prices. A double click returns to the present; on the price axis, it fits the prices again.
    • Style, on each chart: candle colours, the chart's background, the grid, and the colours of volume, profiles, the value area, the point of control, buys and sells, with the value area's share, row sizes and TPO periods. Only the chart changes; the page keeps its own colours.
    • Lines: horizontal lines and trend lines, in a colour you choose, kept with each file. Click a line to select it, drag to move it, Delete to remove it; right-click a chart for a line at that price.
    • Play speeds are now times real time, from 1× to 3,600×, skipping the daily break and weekends. Next trade, Next minute and Next chart bar step forward by hand.
    • Keys in Replay: Space plays and pauses; the Right arrow moves one trade (with a file of bars, one minute), and with Shift, one bar of the first chart; Delete removes the selected line; Escape stops a line half drawn.

    Sources · Atlas

    Where Atlas's figures come from

    Every figure in Atlas comes from an official publisher. This app fetches it on your computer, from the addresses below and nowhere else, with no account, key or sign-up. Each figure is shown as published, with its publisher and the day it is for; anything worked out here (a change, a spread, a share, a rate against the dollar) says so.

    Fetching is on by default. Settings, under Atlas, switches it off; the pages then show the last copies and fetch nothing. A source is fetched only while one of its pages is open, and only when the last copy is older than 12 hours (24 hours for the weekly reports on Positioning and Energy), or when you press Fetch again, at most once every ten minutes. Treasury's yield files for past years are kept once fetched.

    Each request says it comes from The Curve Terminal, with the version and thecurve.in, and carries nothing about you, your journal or your settings. As with any web request, the publisher sees your internet address.

    US Treasury: daily yield curves

    • Daily par yield curve rates and daily par real yield curve rates, one file per year. Used on Rates.
    • home.treasury.gov/resource-center/data-chart-center/interest-rates/daily-treasury-rates.csv/<year>/all?type=daily_treasury_yield_curve, and the same with type=daily_treasury_real_yield_curve.
    • A work of the US government, in the public domain.

    US Treasury, Fiscal Data: auction results

    • Each auction's offering, bids, clearing rate and bidder groups, and auctions announced but not yet held. Used on Auctions and Releases.
    • api.fiscaldata.treasury.gov/services/api/fiscal_service/v1/accounting/od/auctions_query
    • A work of the US government, in the public domain.

    Federal Reserve Bank of New York: reference rates

    • SOFR, EFFR (with the fed funds target range), OBFR, TGCR and BGCR: the latest day, and the last year of EFFR and SOFR. Used on Rates.
    • markets.newyorkfed.org/api/rates/all/latest.json, markets.newyorkfed.org/api/rates/unsecured/effr/search.json and markets.newyorkfed.org/api/rates/secured/sofr/search.json
    • The EFFR, OBFR, SOFR, TGCR and BGCR data are subject to the Terms of Use posted at newyorkfed.org. The New York Fed is not responsible for publication of these rates by The Curve Terminal, does not sanction or endorse any particular republication, and has no liability for your use. The Curve Terminal is not affiliated with the New York Fed. ·

    CFTC: Commitments of Traders

    • Weekly futures positions by trader group, from the Traders in Financial Futures report and the Disaggregated report, futures only. Used on Positioning.
    • publicreporting.cftc.gov/resource/gpe5-46if.json and publicreporting.cftc.gov/resource/72hh-3qpy.json, asking for one market at a time.
    • A work of the US government, in the public domain.

    European Central Bank: euro reference rates

    • The euro against each currency the ECB quotes, for the last 90 days. Used on Currencies.
    • www.ecb.europa.eu/stats/eurofxref/eurofxref-hist-90d.xml
    • Source: European Central Bank. The rates are free of charge on the ECB's website and are published for information only. The daily changes, the 90-day low and high, and the rates per US dollar are worked out here from the ECB's figures. ·

    US Energy Information Administration: weekly stocks

    • Weekly US stocks of commercial crude oil, the Strategic Petroleum Reserve, total gasoline and distillate fuel oil, with EIA's release dates. Used on Energy.

    • A work of the US government. Opening a release takes you to it on federalreserve.gov.

    Privacy · What is stored and sent

    What this app keeps, and what it sends

    On this computer

    • Your journal, in journal.json in the app's folder at %LOCALAPPDATA%\in.thecurve.terminal: every entry you save, with everything you typed into it, and your accounts with their rules, fees and payouts. The version before your last change is kept beside it as journal.bak.json. Settings, under Journal, can show you the file.
    • thecurve.terminal.settings: your settings. Text size, contrast, movement, background, shooting stars, the opening animation, whether Discord status is on, whether Atlas fetches public data, whether hidden marks show while you write in the Folio, in Replay the file last opened, the chart layout, each chart's timeframe, type, panes, profiles, indicators, TPO settings and colours, the playing speeds, the colour of new lines, and the contracts, bracket presets and fill rules last used, whether simulated trades are shown, when the trading day starts, how results are shown, your default costs, your daily loss limit, your monthly fees, and the time zone you last typed times in.
    • The stats are worked out on this computer each time you open them. Nothing about them is stored or sent.
    • Replay, in the replay folder inside the app's folder: a compact copy of each file of bars or trades you load (for bars, their times and prices and volume; for trades, each trade's time, price, size and whether it was at the bid or the ask; your original export is not changed), what each file is (its name, instrument, time zone and the exports joined into it), the lines you draw on each file's charts, and the practice log of trades placed while replaying. The data comes from your own data provider and stays on this computer; Replay can remove a file's copy.
    • Your Folio, in folio.json in the same folder: every page, with its title, text, formatting, tags and marks. The version before your last change is kept as folio.bak.json. Screenshots you add are kept as image files in folio-images beside it, made smaller where they are very large; a screenshot no page uses any more is deleted. Settings, under Folio, can show you the file.
    • Atlas's copies, in the backdrop folder inside the app's folder: the last copy of each public source the app has fetched (Treasury yield files, auction results, reference rates, Commitments of Traders, euro rates, EIA stocks and the Fed's press feed), so its pages open without waiting and work offline. Home shows the latest Commitments of Traders figures for NQ from that copy and fetches nothing itself. They hold public figures only. Settings, under Atlas, deletes them.
    • thecurve.terminal.seen: the last version whose What's new you opened, so the app can mark the link when there is something new.
    • Microsoft Edge WebView2, which draws the window, keeps its own cache in the same folder.

    In Windows

    • If "Open when Windows starts" is on: one value named TheCurveTerminal under HKEY_CURRENT_USER\Software\Microsoft\Windows\CurrentVersion\Run, pointing at the app. Switching it off removes it.
    • Setup records the app under HKEY_CURRENT_USER\Software\Microsoft\Windows\CurrentVersion\Uninstall\TheCurveTerminal, so it appears in Installed apps and can be removed from there.

    What leaves this computer

    • Discord status, while it is on. It is on by default. The app talks only to the Discord app already running on this computer. Discord then shows on your profile: "The Curve Terminal", one short line from a fixed list of twenty (for example "Reading an old trade"), how long the app has been open, and a button that links to thecurve.in. Anyone who can see your Discord activity sees this. Discord's own activity privacy settings apply.
    • The status never includes your trades, your journal, your settings or anything you write. The lines are chosen at random and do not describe what you are doing.
    • To stop it, switch off "Show activity on Discord" in Settings. The status clears straight away.
    • Atlas's requests for public data, while fetching is on. It is on by default. While one of its pages is open, the app asks official publishers for their published figures: the US Treasury (home.treasury.gov and api.fiscaldata.treasury.gov), the Federal Reserve Bank of New York (markets.newyorkfed.org), the CFTC (publicreporting.cftc.gov), the European Central Bank (www.ecb.europa.eu), the US Energy Information Administration (www.eia.gov) and the Federal Reserve Board (www.federalreserve.gov). Sources, at the foot of every page, lists every address and how often each is asked.
    • Those requests say they come from The Curve Terminal, with its version and thecurve.in, and carry nothing about you: no journal, no settings, no identifier. Like any web request, they show each publisher your internet address. To stop them, switch off "Fetch public data" in Settings.
    • Nothing else. No analytics, no tracking, no advertising, no accounts and no update checks. Your journal, your Folio and your Replay files are never sent anywhere.
    • The links to thecurve.in and the Discord community open in your browser, and only when you press them.

    Removing it

    • Uninstalling from Installed apps removes the app, its shortcuts and its startup entry. Tick "Also delete my settings" to remove its folder as well, which includes your journal.
